## Legal basis under U.S. law

[17 U.S.C. § 102(b)](https://www.copyright.gov/title17/92chap1.html#102)
excludes ideas, procedures, processes, systems, and methods of operation from
copyright protection. Copyright can still protect the particular expression in
software code.

[17 U.S.C. § 107](https://www.copyright.gov/title17/92chap1.html#107)
recognizes fair use, including uses for teaching, scholarship, and research.
Courts assess the purpose and character of the use, the nature of the work, the
amount used, and the effect on the potential market. Research purpose and
clean-room procedures inform the analysis; neither creates an automatic exemption.

Two decisions support fair use for necessary intermediate copying during
software reverse engineering:

- *Sega Enterprises Ltd. v. Accolade, Inc.*, 977 F.2d 1510 (9th Cir. 1992),
  recognized fair use for disassembly needed to understand unprotected functional
  elements for a legitimate compatibility purpose. See the
  [U.S. Copyright Office summary](https://www.copyright.gov/fair-use/summaries/segaenters-accolade-9thcir1992.pdf).
- *Sony Computer Entertainment, Inc. v. Connectix Corp.*, 203 F.3d 596
  (9th Cir. 2000), recognized fair use for intermediate BIOS copies needed to
  develop a non-infringing emulator. The final product's absence of infringing
  material mattered to the court. See the
  [U.S. Copyright Office summary](https://www.copyright.gov/fair-use/summaries/sony-connectix-9thcir2000.pdf).

These decisions support the legal basis described here. They do not establish
that every reverse-engineering project or publication of a complete implementation
is fair use.

## Scope of research and learning use

This repository is intended for studying software architecture, inspecting
behavior, conducting experiments, and learning about implementation techniques.
The maintainer's statement concerns the project and uses reviewed by counsel.
It does not determine the legality of every downstream use or grant a general
right to redistribute third-party code or deploy a substitute product.

Copyright fair use and access restrictions are separate questions.
[17 U.S.C. § 1201(f)](https://www.copyright.gov/title17/92chap12.html#1201)
contains a limited interoperability exception with specific conditions. This
notice grants no permission to bypass access controls, obtain unauthorized access,
or disregard applicable agreements. Uses outside the reviewed scope require
their own assessment under the applicable law.
